A share link lets a person with no account on your server watch or read one specific thing. It does not give them a library, search or a profile. The link is the credential: anybody holding it can use it, so Valence lets you decide how long it lasts, how many people may use it and lets you withdraw it at any moment.

## What can be shared

Three kinds of thing can sit behind a link.

| Kind   | What the guest can reach                                                        |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Item   | That one film, episode or video and nothing else.                               |
| Series | Every episode of that programme and nothing outside it.                         |
| Book   | That one book, for reading. Where the guest is up to stays on their own device. |

A guest cannot browse to anything else. Valence names the routes a guest may use and refuses every other one, so a feature added later stays closed to share links until somebody deliberately opens it. Music and other library kinds are not offered for sharing.

Sharing needs the `sharing.link` permission. The default Member, Manager and Administrator roles have it, and Restricted does not. See [Accounts and roles](/use/accounts-and-roles) to change that.

## Creating a link

1. Open the title you want to share and press its Share action. Films, episodes, series and books all have one.
2. If you opened an episode, choose **Just this episode** or **The whole programme**. For a book the choice is **The whole book**.
3. Under **Lasts**, pick how long it should work: **A day**, **Three days**, **A week** (the default), **A month** or **Until I withdraw it**.
4. Under **Who can watch** (or **Who can read** for a book), pick **Anybody with the link**, **One person**, **Two people** or **Five people**.
5. Press the button to make the link, then use **Copy the link** and send it however you like.

<Callout title="The link is shown once" tone="warning">
  The token in the link is stored only as a hash, so Valence cannot show it to you again later. If
  you lose it, withdraw the link and make a new one.
</Callout>

If you cannot see the item because your account lacks access to its library, or you lack `sharing.link`, the dialog tells you it could not be shared and nothing is created.

## How the limits work

Expiry and the person cap are both checked on every request the guest makes, not only when they first open the link. That is what makes them reliable.

- **Expiry.** The link stops working at the moment it expires, including for somebody halfway through a film.
- **Person cap.** The cap counts people, not requests. A guest is recognised by a cookie, so the one person a one person link was made for is not locked out after they arrive. A second, different browser is turned away once the cap is full.
- **Guest memory.** The cookie lasts up to 30 days, but never longer than the link itself.

A link ends in one of three ways, and the guest sees which one:

| Ending    | What the guest is told                                              |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Withdrawn | This link was withdrawn. Somebody stopped it working.               |
| Expired   | This link has expired.                                              |
| Used up   | This link has been used up. It was made for a set number of people. |

If a link is both withdrawn and past its expiry, it is reported as withdrawn.

## What a guest sees

The guest opens an address of the form `/share/` followed by the token. They land on a page showing the title and its episodes, or the book, and can play or read from there. Their playback shows up in the admin Sessions view marked as a guest of whoever made the link.

Somebody who is already signed in to your server is not given extra rights by a link. The share gate only applies to requests with no session, so it is a way in for people without an account, not a second set of permissions.

## Managing your own links

Your links are listed under **Your links** in your account area. Each row shows what the link points at, whether it covers a whole series, its standing and how many times it has been opened.

| Standing    | Meaning                                              |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Live        | It works now. An end date, if any, is shown beneath. |
| Withdrawn   | You or an administrator stopped it.                  |
| Ran out     | Its end date has passed.                             |
| All used up | Its person cap was reached.                          |

To withdraw a link, press the withdraw action on its row and confirm. It stops at once, including for a guest who is watching through it now. The action is hidden once a link is already used up.

## Managing everybody's links

An account with `sharing.manage` (Managers and Administrators by default) sees the **Links** panel under Activity in the admin area. It lists every link on the server, adds a **Handed out by** column and lets you withdraw anybody's.

When you withdraw somebody else's link, Valence confirms it with a toast reading "Withdrew the link." and sends the person who made it a notification saying which link was withdrawn and by whom. That is deliberate: a link disappearing without a word makes a shared server feel arbitrary. The guest, by contrast, is only told that the link was withdrawn, not who did it. The notification appears in the bell described in [Notifications and webhooks](/use/notifications-and-webhooks).

## Sharing versus watch parties

A share link is for one person or a few, watching on their own. A watch party is for people with accounts who watch together in sync. They use separate permissions (`sharing.link` and `sharing.party`). Parties are covered in [Playing media](/use/playing-media).

## Security notes

- Tokens are 32 random bytes, encoded to be URL safe, and are stored hashed.
- Send a link only to people you trust with the content, and prefer an end date over **Until I withdraw it** for anything sensitive.
- If your server is reachable from the internet, guests need it behind HTTPS. See [Reverse proxy](/install/reverse-proxy) and [Security](/reference/security).
