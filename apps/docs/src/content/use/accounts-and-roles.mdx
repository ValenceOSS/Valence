Valence separates three things that are easy to blur together. An **account** is a sign-in: a username or an email address, and a password (or a passkey). A **profile** is a face on the couch: a name, a picture and its own watch history. A **role** is a named bundle of permissions that decides what an account may do on the server. One account can carry several profiles, and one account can hold several roles.

This page covers the People section of the admin area, and the account settings every signed-in person gets for themselves. If you have not created your first administrator yet, start with [First run](/use/first-run).

## Where things live

Open the admin area from the account menu. Under the **People** heading there are two panels.

| Panel    | What it is for                                                                                                                                      |
| -------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Accounts | Adding people, handing out setup links, editing, banning and deleting accounts; ending sessions; choosing roles and library access for each person. |
| Roles    | Creating roles, choosing the permissions each one grants, and seeing who holds it.                                                                  |

Everything on these panels is gated by a permission, so somebody with a narrower role sees fewer panels. The permissions involved are listed later on this page.

![The Accounts panel in the admin area, listing each person with their roles and an actions menu.](/screenshots/admin-accounts.png)

## Accounts

### Usernames

Every account has a **username**, and people sign in with it or with their email address, whichever they type. An email address is optional: an account can have a username and nothing else.

A username is 3 to 30 letters, digits, dots and underscores, and no two accounts share one, whatever the case. When you add somebody without choosing one, Valence makes one from their address or their name, and they may change it when they set their account up. Accounts made before usernames existed were given one the same way, the first time the server started, so everybody can sign in with one.

### Adding a person

You need no more than a name. Anything else you leave out, the person chooses for themselves.

1. Open **Accounts** and choose **Add account**.
2. Enter a **Name**. A **Username** and an **Email** are optional; the username is checked as you type.
3. Under **How they sign in**, choose **Send them a setup link** and how long it works for: **1 day**, **7 days** or **30 days**. Or choose **Set a password for them** and type one, at least ten characters.
4. Choose **Add**.

With a setup link, Valence shows the link and a code to scan with a phone. **Copy link** copies it, and **Send by email** emails it when [email](/use/email) is set up to send setup links and the account has an address. Hand it over however suits you. Choose **Edit account** to go straight on to roles and libraries.

A new account is given the default role, **Member**, so it can watch, share and request. If you would rather somebody start narrower, give them the **Restricted** role instead and remove Member (see [Roles](#roles)).

### Setup links

A setup link is the way into an account nobody has set up yet. Whoever opens it is welcomed by name and asked for what was left blank: a username (filled in with the one Valence chose), an email address if the account has none, and a password, or a passkey on a device that can make one. **Finish** signs them in.

- **Until it is used, nobody can sign in to the account.** It has no password and no passkey, and the placeholder Valence keeps behind an account without an address is never shown, emailed or accepted at sign-in.
- **It works once**, and stops working when it runs out, when you revoke it, or when you make a new one.
- **Valence keeps no copy of it**, only a fingerprint, so it is shown once, when it is made. The Accounts page keeps the links you make while it is open, under **Copy link**. After that, **New link** makes another and the old one stops working.
- The Accounts list says where each account stands: **Waiting for setup** with how long its link has left, **Link expired** when it ran out unused, and **Can't sign in yet** for an account with no password, no passkey and no working link. The filter above the list shows just those accounts.

A setup link also works for an account already in use, as a friendlier password reset: choose **Send a setup link** from its actions menu, or **Create setup link** on its **Signing in** tab. The person keeps their username and address, chooses a new password or makes a passkey, and every session the account held ends. You never learn the password. An account with two-step sign in is not signed in by the link; its owner signs in as usual with the new password and their code.

<Callout title="Prefer a link to a password" tone="quiet">
  A setup link means you never know the password, and a link that leaks after it has been used is
  worthless. If you do hand out a password, it must be at least ten characters; ask the person to
  change it the first time they sign in.
</Callout>

### Editing an account

Choose a name in the list, or the actions menu on a row and then **Edit account**. The editor has five tabs. Changes to the name, username, address, picture, roles and libraries apply when you save. Everything on the Signing in tab happens at once.

| Tab        | What you can do                                                                                          |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Profile    | Change the name, username and address, and set the account's picture. Leave the address empty for none.  |
| Signing in | Make, email or revoke a setup link; set a password; sign the account out everywhere; ban or let back in. |
| Devices    | See every place the account is signed in, with an address and when each session started and expires.     |
| Roles      | Switch each role on or off for this account.                                                             |
| Libraries  | Choose which libraries the account may see, and how old the content in each may be.                      |

### Setting a password and ending sessions

On the **Signing in** tab, **Set a password** sets a new password directly and ends every session the account holds, so the person has to sign in again with the new one. A setup link (above) does the same without you knowing the password. **Sign out everywhere** ends every session without touching the password. Use it when a phone goes missing or somebody is shown the door but should keep their account.

Both need the `account.security` permission. Valence asks you to confirm each before it acts.

### Banning and deleting

The actions menu offers **Ban** and **Delete account**.

- **Ban** asks what to tell the person, then signs them out and refuses them entry until you choose **Unban**. The reason shows under **Banned** in the list. Nothing they own is touched, which makes it the right tool for a pause.
- **Delete account** removes the account, every profile on it and everything those profiles were watching. The person's API keys and share links are revoked at once, so anything using one stops working. Playlists they shared with the household stay, marked as belonging to a removed profile. Nothing leaves your libraries. This cannot be undone.

Banning needs `account.ban`. Deleting and editing need `account.manage`. Adding an account needs `account.invite`, and making a setup link for one needs `account.invite` or `account.security`.

### Who may act on whom

Valence protects itself from one administrator acting against another. The rules are the same everywhere in the admin area.

- Nobody can ban, delete or reset themselves. Doing so would remove their own last way in.
- Every role has a **rank**, a number. You cannot act on an account whose highest role is at or above your own, and you cannot change a role at or above yours. This applies to administrators too.
- You cannot grant a permission you do not hold yourself.
- The **owner** sits outside these rules. Nobody can act on the owner, and the owner can act on anybody. On a server that predates the idea of an owner, the oldest administrator is recorded as the owner the first time it starts, which on every install is the account made during setup.

The practical result is that giving somebody the Administrator role lets them help run the server but does not let them lock you out of it.

## Roles

### The default roles

A fresh server is given four roles the first time it starts. Their ranks are 300, 200, 100 and 0. Valence records that it has seeded them, so if you delete a role it does not come back on the next restart. Accounts that already exist when the roles are first created are assigned one: administrators get Administrator, everyone else gets Member.

| Role          | Rank | What it grants                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------- | ---- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Administrator | 300  | Everything, including permissions added by later releases.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Manager       | 200  | Looks after libraries and what is in them, but not the server itself: creating and editing libraries, running and scheduling jobs, rescanning, correcting metadata and artwork, hiding items, seeing and stopping streams, share management, all the request permissions except setting up requesting, API keys, and reading the logs and monitor. |
| Member        | 100  | Sharing links, starting watch parties, downloading media, asking for films, series and music, and seeing everybody's requests and how their downloads are going.                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Restricted    | 0    | Watching, and nothing else.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |

Everyone can watch what their libraries allow. Roles only add abilities on top of that.

### Creating and editing a role

1. Open **Roles** and choose **Create a role**.
2. Give it a **Name**, a **Rank** and a **Colour**. The colour is shown wherever someone holding the role appears.
3. Create it, then open **Edit role** to choose permissions.

The role editor has three tabs: **Display**, **Permissions** and **Manage members**. Each permission is described in a sentence, so you can read what you are granting before you switch it on. A higher rank manages a lower one, and nobody may touch a role at or above their own.

![The role editor open on the Permissions tab, showing the grouped permission list with a description beside each.](/screenshots/admin-role-permissions.png)

Deleting a role removes what it granted from everyone who held it. Creating roles needs `account.roles`.

### The permission catalogue

Permissions are the only thing a role contains. These are all of them.

| Permission             | What it lets somebody do                                                                                      |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `administrator`        | Everything, including anything added later.                                                                   |
| `library.create`       | Add a library.                                                                                                |
| `library.edit`         | Change a library's settings.                                                                                  |
| `library.delete`       | Remove a library and forget everything scanned from it.                                                       |
| `jobs.run`             | Start a job by hand, stop one, and change how many run at once.                                               |
| `jobs.schedule`        | Change when a recurring job runs.                                                                             |
| `jobs.runDestructive`  | Run reset and rebuild, the jobs that throw away what is stored.                                               |
| `media.rescan`         | Ask Valence to read one item again.                                                                           |
| `media.delete`         | Delete a media file from disk.                                                                                |
| `media.reencode`       | Re-encode media at a chosen quality.                                                                          |
| `media.override`       | Correct an item's metadata by hand.                                                                           |
| `media.artwork`        | Replace an item's poster or backdrop.                                                                         |
| `media.hide`           | Hide an item from every profile.                                                                              |
| `sharing.link`         | Create share links.                                                                                           |
| `sharing.party`        | Start watch parties.                                                                                          |
| `sharing.manage`       | See every share link and withdraw any of them.                                                                |
| `streaming.view`       | See who is watching and what.                                                                                 |
| `streaming.stop`       | End somebody else's stream.                                                                                   |
| `streaming.pause`      | Pause somebody else's stream.                                                                                 |
| `streaming.message`    | Send a message that appears over somebody else's stream.                                                      |
| `download.media`       | Save media to a device for offline viewing.                                                                   |
| `requests.ask`         | Request films and series.                                                                                     |
| `requests.askMusic`    | Request artists and albums.                                                                                   |
| `requests.autoApprove` | Have requests go straight through without approval.                                                           |
| `requests.viewAll`     | See everybody's requests.                                                                                     |
| `requests.approve`     | Approve or refuse other people's requests.                                                                    |
| `requests.manage`      | Set up indexers, download clients and quality profiles.                                                       |
| `account.invite`       | Add a new account and hand out its setup link.                                                                |
| `account.manage`       | List, rename and remove accounts.                                                                             |
| `account.ban`          | Ban and unban accounts.                                                                                       |
| `account.roles`        | Create roles and choose what they grant.                                                                      |
| `account.profiles`     | Add, rename or remove another account's profiles.                                                             |
| `account.security`     | Set passwords, send setup links as resets, end sessions and see where an account is signed in.                |
| `account.keys`         | Hold API keys.                                                                                                |
| `server.settings`      | Change server-wide settings.                                                                                  |
| `server.backup`        | Start a backup or restore one.                                                                                |
| `server.logs`          | Read the server's log.                                                                                        |
| `server.monitor`       | See what the server is doing: load, memory and encoding.                                                      |
| `server.plugins`       | Install plugins from the catalogue or a file, choose what they may do, change their settings and remove them. |
| `server.webhooks`      | Have the server call out to another address when something happens.                                           |

Installed plugins can add permissions of their own, such as `plugin.anilist.sync`. They appear in the role editor in a group named after each plugin, described by the plugin, and are granted like any other. The Administrator role holds every one of them. They go when the plugin is removed. See [plugins](/use/plugins#permissions-a-plugin-adds).

An account's effective permissions are the union of everything its roles grant. The server also supports per-account allow and deny overrides for a single permission through the API, but the admin area does not expose them yet, so manage access through roles.

## Limiting what somebody can see

The **Libraries** tab in the account editor controls reach per account. Every library is listed, and by default the account may see all of them.

- Click a library's button to switch it off for that account. A person who can reach no library at all sees an empty server, which looks broken rather than restricted, so Valence warns you.
- With a library switched on, an **age limit** menu lets you cap what may appear from that library by certification. Choose "Nothing above" plus a limit, or leave it open.
- **Allow unrated** decides whether items with no certification appear under a limit. It only appears once a limit is set.

Limits belong to the account and therefore to every profile on it. If a parent and a child share one account, give the child an account of their own and limit that one.

## Profiles and the household

The household is the account itself, presented to the family: it has a name, a colour and a picture, set up the first time somebody signs in. Under it sit the profiles, each of which keeps its own history, progress, favourites, ratings and hidden items.

After signing in, a **profile picker** asks who is watching. From it you can:

- Add a profile, giving it a name (up to 24 characters), a colour and a picture. A picture can be an uploaded photograph, one of the drawn faces, or simply the initial of the name.
- Edit a profile: name, colour, picture, how many episodes play before Valence asks "are you still watching", and whether the title you are playing is shown on Discord.
- Remove a profile. Everything that profile watched goes with it: history, progress, likes, ratings and hidden items. Playlists it shared with the household stay, and private ones go.

Administrators with `account.profiles` can manage other people's profiles on their behalf.

## Your own account

Everyone gets an account area with six panels.

| Panel    | What it holds                                                                                                                                     |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Profile  | Display name, profile picture, theme (kept on this device), motion preference, colour, the still-watching prompt and the Discord presence option. |
| Security | Sign-in settings: two-step sign in, passkeys and, for those allowed, API keys.                                                                    |
| Devices  | Every device signed in to the account, with a way to sign each out.                                                                               |
| Links    | Share links you have created, with a way to withdraw each. See [Sharing](/use/sharing).                                                           |
| History  | What you have watched.                                                                                                                            |
| Hidden   | What you have hidden, and whole libraries you have hidden, with a way to bring them back.                                                         |

### Sessions

A sign-in lasts 30 days and is refreshed once a day while you use it. Sign-out on one device does not affect the others. The Devices panel lists every live session so that you can end any of them.

### Two-step sign in

On **Security**, enable **Two-factor authentication** to require a code from an authenticator app as well as your password. You confirm your password, scan the QR code (or type the setup key), save the backup codes, then enter a code from your app to finish. Two-step sign in is not on until you enter that first code.

### Passkeys

Passkeys let you sign in with the face, fingerprint or PIN a device already uses, instead of a password. Add one from **Security**, give it a name you will recognise, such as the device you are on, and rename or remove it later. Passkeys need a browser and device that support them; where they are unavailable the option says so.

In the desktop app, **Use a passkey** works like this:

- **Windows** opens Windows Hello inside the app, which also offers a phone, a security key or any passkey manager Windows knows about. You can add passkeys from **Security** as you would in a browser.
- **macOS** and **Linux** open Valence's sign-in page in your default browser, so the passkeys on offer are the ones your browser has: iCloud Keychain in Safari, or whichever password manager extension you use. Once you are signed in, the browser hands you back to the app. You add passkeys from your browser too; **Security** has a button that opens it there.

Passkeys in the desktop app need your server reached over HTTPS, the same as in a browser.

### API keys

If your role includes `account.keys`, the **API keys** card on the Security panel lets you create keys for scripts and devices outside the browser. Give a key a name and create it. The key is shown once, so copy it then. You can turn a key off and on again, or revoke it. A key can be created with an expiry, a narrower set of permissions and a rate limit through the API, and it can never carry a permission that its holder does not have. See the [API overview](/reference/api-overview) for how to use one.
