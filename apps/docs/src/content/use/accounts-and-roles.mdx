Valence separates three things that are easy to blur together. An **account** is a sign-in: a username or an email address, and a password (or a passkey). A **profile** is a face on the couch: a name, a picture and its own watch history. A **role** is a named bundle of permissions that decides what an account may do on the server. One account can carry several profiles, and one account can hold several roles.

This page covers the People section of the admin area, and the account settings every signed-in person gets for themselves. If you have not created your first administrator yet, start with [First run](/use/first-run).

## Where things live

Open the admin area from the account menu. Under the **People** heading there are two panels.

| Panel    | What it is for                                                                                                                                      |
| -------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Accounts | Adding people, handing out setup links, editing, banning and deleting accounts; ending sessions; choosing roles and library access for each person. |
| Roles    | Creating roles, choosing the permissions each one grants, and seeing who holds it.                                                                  |

Everything on these panels is gated by a permission, so somebody with a narrower role sees fewer panels. The permissions involved are listed later on this page.

![The Accounts panel in the admin area, listing each person with their roles and an actions menu.](/screenshots/admin-accounts.png)

## Accounts

### Usernames

Every account has a **username**, and people sign in with it or with their email address, whichever they type. An email address is optional: an account can have a username and nothing else.

A username is 3 to 30 letters, digits, dots and underscores, and no two accounts share one, whatever the case. When you add somebody without choosing one, Valence makes one from their address or their name, and they may change it when they set their account up. Accounts made before usernames existed were given one the same way, the first time the server started, so everybody can sign in with one.

### Adding a person

You need no more than a name. Anything else you leave out, the person chooses for themselves.

1. Open **Accounts** and choose **Add account**.
2. Enter a **Name**. A **Username** and an **Email** are optional; the username is checked as you type.
3. Under **How they sign in**, choose **Send them a setup link** and how long it works for: **1 day**, **7 days** or **30 days**. Or choose **Set a password for them** and type one, at least ten characters.
4. Choose **Add**.

With a setup link, Valence shows the link and a code to scan with a phone. **Copy link** copies it, and **Send by email** emails it when [email](/use/email) is set up to send setup links and the account has an address. Hand it over however suits you. Choose **Edit account** to go straight on to roles and libraries.

A new account is given the default role, **Member**, so it can watch, share and request. If you would rather somebody start narrower, give them the **Restricted** role instead and remove Member (see [Roles](#roles)).

### Setup links

A setup link is the way into an account nobody has set up yet. Whoever opens it is welcomed by name and asked for what was left blank: a username (filled in with the one Valence chose), an email address if the account has none, and a password, or a passkey on a device that can make one. **Finish** signs them in.

- **Until it is used, nobody can sign in to the account.** It has no password and no passkey, and the placeholder Valence keeps behind an account without an address is never shown, emailed or accepted at sign-in.
- **It works once**, and stops working when it runs out, when you revoke it, or when you make a new one.
- **Valence keeps no copy of it**, only a fingerprint, so it is shown once, when it is made. The Accounts page keeps the links you make while it is open, under **Copy link**. After that, **New link** makes another and the old one stops working.
- The Accounts list says where each account stands: **Waiting for setup** with how long its link has left, **Link expired** when it ran out unused, and **Can't sign in yet** for an account with no password, no passkey and no working link. The filter above the list shows just those accounts.

A setup link also works for an account already in use, as a friendlier password reset: choose **Send a setup link** from its actions menu, or **Create setup link** on its **Signing in** tab. The person keeps their username and address, chooses a new password or makes a passkey, and every session the account held ends. You never learn the password. An account with two-step sign in is not signed in by the link; its owner signs in as usual with the new password and their code.

<Callout title="Prefer a link to a password" tone="quiet">
  A setup link means you never know the password, and a link that leaks after it has been used is
  worthless. If you do hand out a password, it must be at least ten characters; ask the person to
  change it the first time they sign in.
</Callout>

### Editing an account

Choose a name in the list, or the actions menu on a row and then **Edit account**. The editor has seven tabs. Changes to the name, username, address, picture, roles and libraries apply when you save. Everything on the Profiles and Signing in tabs happens at once.

| Tab        | What you can do                                                                                                         |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Profile    | Change the name, username and address, and set the account's picture. Leave the address empty for none.                 |
| Profiles   | Add, rename and remove the account's profiles, clear a PIN, hand over ownership and limit a profile.                    |
| Signing in | Make, email or revoke a setup link; set a password; sign the account out everywhere; ban or let back in.                |
| Devices    | See every device the account is signed in on, what each reaches Valence through, its address and when it was last used. |
| Roles      | Switch each role on or off for this account.                                                                            |
| Libraries  | Choose which libraries the account may see, and how old the content in each may be.                                     |
| Streaming  | Set the account's own streaming limits.                                                                                 |

### Streaming limits

The **Streaming** tab of an account sets its own limits, and the **Streaming** tab of a role sets the same limits for everyone who holds it. Each can be left with no limit.

- **Highest quality**: **No limit**, **4K**, **1440p**, **1080p**, **720p**, **480p** or **360p**. Anything above it is sent at that quality instead.
- **Limit downloads too**, shown once a quality is chosen: downloads are saved at that quality or lower as well.
- **Highest bitrate**, in Mbps: everything played is sent at or below it. This is useful when the server's upload is slow.
- **Streams at once**: how many things may play at the same time, counting every stream on the account, across all its profiles and devices. Starting one more is refused with a message saying how many it may have, and asking for one of the others to be stopped. Changing what plays on a device that is already streaming does not count twice.

Lower limits mean more of what is played has to be transcoded, which puts more load on the server's CPU or GPU.

An account streams under the limits of its highest role, and its own limits can only make them stricter. Where its highest role holds it to less than its own limit, the account's tab says so; the tab also shows how many streams the account has running now. Players grey out the qualities above the limit, with a lock, and asking for the file directly is refused when it is above the limit. On the **Sessions** page, each session of a limited account says how many of its streams are running out of how many, and the bitrate it is held to.

An account's limits are read and set at `/api/admin/accounts/{userId}/stream-limit`, and a role's at `/api/admin/roles/{roleId}/stream-limit`. Both need `account.manage`. `GET /api/account/stream-limit` returns the limits the signed-in account streams under.

### Setting a password and ending sessions

On the **Signing in** tab, **Set a password** sets a new password directly and ends every session the account holds, so the person has to sign in again with the new one. A setup link (above) does the same without you knowing the password. **Sign out everywhere** ends every session without touching the password. Use it when a phone goes missing or somebody is shown the door but should keep their account.

A single device can be signed out from the account's **Devices** tab, or from the **Sessions** page while it has Valence open, where each session's actions include **Sign out this device**. Either way, every sign-in that device holds is ended.

Whichever way sign-ins end (one device, a new password, **Sign out everywhere**, a ban or deleting the account), any device still open on them stops what it is playing and goes back to the sign-in screen. A video that was playing says why it stopped. A device on an API key has no sign-in to end: revoke the key instead.

Hovering a device's name on the **Devices** tab or the **Sessions** page shows the raw user agent it sent, for when the name Valence worked out from it is not enough.

All of these need the `account.manage` permission. Valence asks you to confirm each before it acts.

### Banning and deleting

The actions menu offers **Ban** and **Delete account**.

- **Ban** asks what to tell the person, then signs them out and refuses them entry until you choose **Unban**. The reason shows under **Banned** in the list. Nothing they own is touched, which makes it the right tool for a pause.
- **Delete account** removes the account, every profile on it and everything those profiles were watching. The person's API keys and share links are revoked at once, so anything using one stops working. Playlists they shared with the household stay, marked as belonging to a removed profile. Their own smart collections go with them, shared or not. Nothing leaves your libraries. This cannot be undone.

Banning, deleting and editing need `account.manage`. Adding an account needs `account.invite`, and making a setup link for one needs `account.invite` or `account.manage`.

### Who may act on whom

Valence protects itself from one administrator acting against another. The rules are the same everywhere in the admin area.

- Nobody can ban, delete or reset themselves. Doing so would remove their own last way in.
- Roles are a hierarchy, ordered highest first on the **Roles** page. You cannot act on an account whose highest role is at or above your own, and you cannot change, move or hand out a role at or above yours. This applies to administrators too.
- You cannot grant a permission you do not hold yourself.
- The **owner** sits outside these rules. Nobody can act on the owner, and the owner can act on anybody. On a server that predates the idea of an owner, the oldest administrator is recorded as the owner the first time it starts, which on every install is the account made during setup.

The practical result is that giving somebody the Administrator role lets them help run the server but does not let them lock you out of it.

## Roles

### The default roles

A fresh server is given four roles the first time it starts, highest first: Administrator, Manager, Member and Restricted. Valence records that it has seeded them, so if you delete a role it does not come back on the next restart. Accounts that already exist when the roles are first created are assigned one: administrators get Administrator, everyone else gets Member.

| Role          | What it grants                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Administrator | Everything, including permissions added by later releases.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Manager       | Looks after libraries and what is in them, but not the server itself: creating and editing libraries, running and scheduling jobs, editing titles, finding subtitles, seeing and controlling streams, share management, all the request permissions except setting up requesting, playing films from sources, API keys, and seeing the server's status and logs. |
| Member        | Sharing links, starting watch parties, downloading media, finding subtitles, asking for films, series and music, seeing everybody's requests and how their downloads are going, and playing films from sources.                                                                                                                                                  |
| Restricted    | Watching, and nothing else.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |

Everyone can watch what their libraries allow. Roles only add abilities on top of that.

### Creating and editing a role

Open **Roles**. The roles are listed down the left, highest first, with how many members each has, and the role you choose opens beside them. Its **Name** and **Colour** sit at the top, and below them are three tabs: **Permissions**, **Members** and **Streaming**. The colour is shown wherever someone holding the role appears.

Changes are not saved as you make them. Once something changes, a bar appears with **Save changes**, which saves everything together, and **Reset**, which puts it back. Leaving a role with changes unsaved asks whether to discard them.

**Create role** starts a new one, called **New role** until you name it. Choose its permissions, members and limits, then save it with **Create role** in the same bar. A new role starts at the bottom of the list.

![The role editor open on the Permissions tab, showing the grouped permission list with a description beside each.](/screenshots/admin-role-permissions.png)

Deleting a role removes what it granted from everyone who held it. A role with every permission can't be deleted. Creating, changing and ordering roles needs `account.roles`.

### Ordering roles

Roles have no numbers. Their order is what counts: whoever holds a higher role can manage the roles below it. Drag a role up or down the list to move it, or use **Move up** and **Move down** in its **⋯** menu. Roles at or above your own show a lock: you can't change or move them, or move another role above them.

Somebody with `account.roles` who is not an administrator can still only hand out what they have themselves:

- the switches for permissions they do not hold are greyed out, so they cannot give them;
- they cannot change a role at or above their own, or give it to anybody.

### The permission catalogue

Permissions are the only thing a role contains. Each is described in a sentence in the role editor, so you can read what you are granting before you switch it on. The everyday permissions are shown first, and the ones about running the server follow under **Admin permissions**. The permissions about requests appear only once requesting is set up.

| Permission             | Shown as                          | What it lets somebody do                                                                                                                          |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `administrator`        | **Everything**                    | Every permission, including ones added in future updates.                                                                                         |
| `download.media`       | **Download media**                | Download media to a device to watch offline.                                                                                                      |
| `media.subtitles`      | **Find subtitles**                | Search for and download subtitles while watching.                                                                                                 |
| `sources.play`         | **Watch films not on the server** | Play a film straight away from a plugin, without requesting or downloading it. Only shown when a plugin offers sources; needs `requests.ask` too. |
| `sharing.link`         | **Create share links**            | Let someone outside the server watch one title through a link.                                                                                    |
| `sharing.party`        | **Start watch parties**           | Play one stream to everyone who joins.                                                                                                            |
| `requests.ask`         | **Request films and series**      | Ask for films and series.                                                                                                                         |
| `requests.askMusic`    | **Request music**                 | Ask for artists and albums.                                                                                                                       |
| `requests.viewAll`     | **See everyone’s requests**       | See everyone's requests and how each download is going.                                                                                           |
| `requests.autoApprove` | **Skip approval**                 | Their requests download straight away, without waiting for approval.                                                                              |
| `account.keys`         | **Create API keys**               | Create API keys for scripts and devices outside the app.                                                                                          |

Under **Admin permissions**:

| Permission            | Shown as                           | What it lets somebody do                                                                                                                                     |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `requests.approve`    | **Approve requests**               | Approve or refuse other people's requests.                                                                                                                   |
| `requests.manage`     | **Set up requests**                | Set up indexers, download clients and quality profiles, and see the VPN status.                                                                              |
| `sharing.manage`      | **Manage share links**             | See and revoke anyone's share links.                                                                                                                         |
| `streaming.view`      | **See who’s watching**             | See who is watching and what.                                                                                                                                |
| `streaming.control`   | **Control other people’s streams** | Pause or stop someone else's stream, or send a message over it.                                                                                              |
| `media.override`      | **Edit titles**                    | Fix wrong matches, edit metadata, choose artwork and rescan titles.                                                                                          |
| `media.reencode`      | **Re-encode media**                | Re-encode media at a chosen quality.                                                                                                                         |
| `media.delete`        | **Delete files**                   | Delete media files from disk, not just from the library.                                                                                                     |
| `library.edit`        | **Manage libraries**               | Add libraries and change their settings, such as names, languages and scan options.                                                                          |
| `library.delete`      | **Delete libraries**               | Remove a library and forget everything scanned from it.                                                                                                      |
| `jobs.run`            | **Run and schedule jobs**          | Start jobs such as scans by hand, and change when they run.                                                                                                  |
| `jobs.runDestructive` | **Reset and rebuild**              | Run reset and rebuild, the jobs that throw away what is stored.                                                                                              |
| `account.invite`      | **Invite people**                  | Add a new account and hand out its setup link.                                                                                                               |
| `account.manage`      | **Manage accounts**                | Rename, suspend, ban or remove accounts, reset passwords, sign people out and manage their profiles.                                                         |
| `account.roles`       | **Manage roles**                   | Create roles, put them in order and choose what they grant.                                                                                                  |
| `server.monitor`      | **See server status and logs**     | See CPU, memory and transcodes, and read the server log.                                                                                                     |
| `server.settings`     | **Change server settings**         | Change server-wide settings, such as hardware acceleration, quality and webhooks.                                                                            |
| `server.plugins`      | **Manage plugins**                 | Install plugins from the catalogue or a file, choose what they may do, change their settings and remove them.                                                |
| `server.links`        | **Link servers**                   | Make and accept invites to link this server with another Valence server, approve or decline link requests, and unlink servers. Linking alone shares nothing. |
| `server.backup`       | **Back up and restore**            | Start a backup or restore one.                                                                                                                               |

Installed plugins can add permissions of their own, such as `plugin.anilist.sync`. They appear in the role editor in a group named after each plugin, described by the plugin, and are granted like any other. The Administrator role holds every one of them. They go when the plugin is removed. See [plugins](/use/plugins#permissions-a-plugin-adds).

An account's effective permissions are the union of everything its roles grant. The server also supports per-account allow and deny overrides for a single permission through the API, but the admin area does not expose them yet, so manage access through roles.

## Limiting what somebody can see

The **Libraries** tab in the account editor controls reach per account. Every library is listed, and by default the account may see all of them.

- Click a library's button to switch it off for that account. A person who can reach no library at all sees an empty server, which looks broken rather than restricted, so Valence warns you.
- With a library switched on, an **age limit** menu lets you cap what may appear from that library by certification. Choose "Nothing above" plus a limit, or leave it open.
- **Allow unrated** decides whether items with no certification appear under a limit. It only appears once a limit is set.

Limits set here belong to the account and therefore to every profile on it. To hold back one person on a shared account, such as a child, the account's owner limits that profile instead: see [Limiting one profile](#limiting-one-profile).

## Profiles and the household

The household is the account itself, presented to the family: it has a name, a colour and a picture, set up the first time somebody signs in. Under it sit up to six profiles, each of which keeps its own history, progress, favourites, ratings, hidden items, share links and requests.

### The owner and everybody else

One profile on each account is its **owner**: the first one made, unless it was handed on. The owner is the only profile that can change the account itself:

- its password, email address, username, two-step sign in, passkeys and API keys;
- the household's name and picture, and the devices signed in to it;
- the other profiles: adding, renaming and removing them, their PINs, and what each may watch.

The owner also holds the account's role. Every other profile on the account can watch, share links, start watch parties, download, see requests and ask for things, and nothing more, however much the account's role grants. An Administrator's partner on the same account is not an administrator.

Each profile can change its own name, picture and preferences, and set or remove its own PIN.

### Choosing who is watching

An account with one profile and no PIN never asks: there is nobody else it could be. Once there are more, every start of the app asks **Who's watching?** after signing in, on every device. In a browser that means each new tab or window. The choice is held by the server for that device, so a profile cannot be chosen by anything other than the account's own sign-in and, where it has one, its PIN.

The account menu lists the account's profiles at the top. Choosing one without a PIN moves to it straight away; choosing one with a PIN asks for it first.

### PINs

A profile with a PIN asks for it when chosen. A PIN is 4 to 8 digits.

- After five wrong PINs in a row, that profile cannot be tried for a minute, then five, fifteen and sixty minutes after each further five. The owner is told each time it happens.
- **Forgot the PIN?** under the PIN field asks for the account's password and a new PIN.
- The owner can set, change or remove any profile's PIN from **Profiles** in their account. An administrator can clear one from the account editor.

### Limiting one profile

The owner can narrow what each other profile may watch from **Profiles → Libraries and ages**: which of the account's libraries it sees, and up to what age in each. A profile is only ever narrowed: it never sees a library or an age its account cannot. **Stop requests** keeps a profile from asking for things.

### Managing other people's profiles

Administrators with `account.manage` manage any account's profiles from the **Profiles** tab of the account editor, with the same rules about who may act on whom as everything else there. From it they can add, rename and remove profiles, clear a forgotten PIN, hand ownership to another profile and narrow what a profile may watch.

**Give a profile an account of its own** (an administrator's action, needing `account.manage`) moves a profile to a new account. It cannot move the owner's profile.

## Your own account

Everyone gets an account area with these panels. Profiles, Security and Devices are shown to the account's owner only.

| Panel    | What it holds                                                                                                                                     |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Profile  | Display name, profile picture, theme (kept on this device), motion preference, colour, the still-watching prompt and the Discord presence option. |
| Security | Sign-in settings: two-step sign in, passkeys and, for those allowed, API keys.                                                                    |
| Profiles | The profiles on the account, their PINs and what each may watch. See [Profiles and the household](#profiles-and-the-household).                   |
| Devices  | Every device signed in to the account, with a way to rename or sign out each one.                                                                 |
| Links    | Share links you have created, with a way to withdraw each. See [Sharing](/use/sharing).                                                           |
| History  | What you have watched.                                                                                                                            |
| Hidden   | What you have hidden, and whole libraries you have hidden, with a way to bring them back.                                                         |

### More than one account on a browser

**Add another account** in the account menu brings up the way in over the app, so a second person can sign in without the first signing out. Up to five accounts can be signed in on one browser at once. Each keeps its own session, profiles, history and settings, and **Switch to** followed by a name in the same menu moves between them. **Sign out** signs every account on that browser out together, so walking away from a shared screen leaves nobody signed in. This is a browser and desktop app feature; the phone and television apps keep one account per server.

### Sessions

A sign-in lasts 30 days and is refreshed once a day while you use it. Sign-out on one device does not affect the others. The Devices panel lists every device signed in to the account so that you can end any of them. Each device is named the way you would recognise it, with what it reaches Valence through beneath: "iPhone 17 Pro Max" and "Valence for iOS", or "Mac" and "Safari on macOS". A device that signs in again, such as a browser signed out and back in, stays one entry rather than becoming a new one, and a device in use right now says so. Use **Rename** on a device to give it a name of your own, such as "Living room laptop". Clearing the name gives it back the one Valence chose.

### Two-step sign in

On **Security**, enable **Two-factor authentication** to require a code from an authenticator app as well as your password. You confirm your password, scan the QR code (or type the setup key), save the backup codes, then enter a code from your app to finish. Two-step sign in is not on until you enter that first code.

### Passkeys

Passkeys let you sign in with the face, fingerprint or PIN a device already uses, instead of a password. Add one from **Security**, give it a name you will recognise, such as the device you are on, and rename or remove it later. Passkeys need a browser and device that support them; where they are unavailable the option says so.

In the desktop app, **Use a passkey** works like this:

- **Windows** opens Windows Hello inside the app, which also offers a phone, a security key or any passkey manager Windows knows about. You can add passkeys from **Security** as you would in a browser.
- **macOS** and **Linux** open Valence's sign-in page in your default browser, so the passkeys on offer are the ones your browser has: iCloud Keychain in Safari, or whichever password manager extension you use. Once you are signed in, the browser hands you back to the app. You add passkeys from your browser too; **Security** has a button that opens it there.

Passkeys in the desktop app need your server reached over HTTPS, the same as in a browser.

### API keys

If your role includes `account.keys`, the **API keys** card on the Security panel lets you create keys for scripts and devices outside the browser. Keys are made and managed from a signed-in browser only: a request made with a key can't list, make, turn off or revoke keys. Give a key a name and create it. The key is shown once, so copy it then. You can turn a key off and on again, or revoke it. A key can be created with an expiry, a narrower set of permissions and a rate limit through the API, and it can never carry a permission that its holder does not have. A key acts as the profile that made it, which is always the account's owner. Keys are made here or through `/api/keys`, never through the sign-in service's own key routes, which Valence refuses. See the [API overview](/reference/api-overview) for how to use one.
