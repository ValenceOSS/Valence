Plugins add to Valence: an anime list that stays in step, playlists brought across from another service, new themes. Installing and managing them is for administrators, from **Admin**, then **Plugins**. This needs the plugins permission, `server.plugins`, which administrators have.

<Callout title="How safe is a plugin?" tone="quiet">
  A plugin's code runs only on your server, in a sandbox of its own, and can reach only what you
  allow it. Nothing from a plugin ever runs in anyone's browser, phone or TV. See [how plugins are
  kept safe](/develop/plugins).
</Callout>

## From the catalogue

The **Catalogue** tab lists the official plugins. Each is signed by the Valence project, and your server checks the signature before it installs anything. A plugin written for a newer version of Valence than yours is marked as not compatible yet.

1. Choose a plugin, then **Review and install**.
2. Read what it asks to be allowed to do. Every permission is said in plain words, for example "Talks to api.spotify.com" or "Reads and updates what people have watched".
3. If you are happy, **Install**.

## From a file

**Upload** takes a `.vplugin` file, for a plugin someone gave you or one you wrote.

<Callout title="Unsigned plugins" tone="warning">
  If the file is not signed by a key your server knows, Valence cannot tell you who made it. It says
  so, and asks you to confirm you trust whoever gave it to you before installing. Only install an
  unsigned plugin from someone you would trust with what it asks for.
</Callout>

If the file is a newer version of a plugin you already have, Valence says which version it replaces, and points out anything it asks for that the installed one did not.

## Reading the permissions

| You will see                                          | It means                                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Talks to a list of addresses                          | It may make `https` requests to exactly those hosts, and nowhere else     |
| Reads your library                                    | It may search and read what is in your libraries, never change it         |
| Reads, or reads and updates, what people have watched | It may read, or also mark, watched progress for the people it acts for    |
| Asks for titles                                       | It may make requests, as the person using it and within their own rights  |
| Reads, or creates and fills, playlists                | It may read, or also make and add to, playlists of the people it acts for |
| Keeps its own data, up to a size                      | A private store for the plugin, removed with it                           |
| Connects to an account elsewhere                      | People may connect their account on that service to the plugin            |
| Sends notifications                                   | It may put notifications in people's bell                                 |
| Receives webhooks                                     | Outside services may send it messages at private addresses of its own     |
| Tells your webhooks things                            | It may send the events it lists to webhooks subscribed to plugin events   |

A plugin acts only for the people who use its page or connect an account to it. It never sees passwords, sessions or files.

## Settings

Some plugins need details from you before they work, such as an OAuth client ID for a service people will connect to. Fill them in on the plugin's **Settings**. Secret settings are stored encrypted and never shown again once saved; type a new value to replace one.

A plugin that connects people's accounts on another service needs you to register an OAuth application with that service. Use this address as its redirect:

```
https://<your Valence address>/api/plugins/oauth/callback
```

The plugin's **Settings** shows this address under **Redirect URL**, ready to copy.

A plugin that receives webhooks lists one private address for each under **Settings** too. Give each to the service that sends it; anyone with the address can post to it, so treat it like a password. Reinstalling the plugin after removing it gives new addresses.

<Callout title="Addresses from outside" tone="warning">
  Both addresses are built from `BETTER_AUTH_URL`. For services outside your home to reach them, it
  must be the address Valence is reached at from the internet, not `localhost` or a local IP.
</Callout>

## Permissions a plugin adds

A plugin may add permissions of its own, such as "Sync lists", to decide who can use its pages and panels. They appear in the [role editor](/use/accounts-and-roles#the-permission-catalogue) in a group named after the plugin, and are granted like any other. The Administrator role holds them all. Removing the plugin takes its permissions out of every role, and installing it again does not bring those grants back.

## What people see

- **Pages** appear in each person's account area, or in the admin area for pages meant for administrators.
- **Panels** appear beside the things they belong to, such as a title or a playlist.
- **Themes** are offered with Valence's own on the theme choice, on the web, the phone and the TV.
- **Scheduled work** appears on the [jobs page](/use/jobs-and-logs), where you can run it now or see how it went. What a plugin logs appears on the logs page, tagged with its name.

## Updating

When the catalogue has a newer version, the plugin's row says so with **Update to** and the version. If the update asks for more than the installed version, you review the difference before it is installed. Some updates convert what the plugin kept to a new shape as they install.

### Rolling back

Valence keeps the version an update replaced, with what it had kept as it was at that moment. **Roll back to** and that version puts it back. Anything the plugin kept after the update is lost, and only one step back is kept: rolling back again, or updating again, starts afresh.

## Turning a plugin off, or removing it

- **The switch** on its row turns a plugin off at once. Its pages, panels and themes disappear and its scheduled work stops, but its settings, data and people's connected accounts are kept for when you turn it back on. Anybody on one of its pages, on the web, the phone or the TV, is taken back to where they came from and told why.
- **Remove** takes the plugin away with its settings, everything it kept, every connected account, its permissions in roles, its webhook addresses and the version kept for rolling back. The dialog first lists what this particular plugin would take with it, such as how much it kept and how many people connected an account to which service, and offers **Disable plugin** instead, which keeps all of that. Where a service supports it, Valence also asks that service to cancel the access people gave, so the plugin disappears from their account there too.

If a plugin keeps failing, Valence stops restarting it and says why on its row. Disabling it and turning it back on starts it afresh.
