Plugins add to Valence: an anime list that stays in step, playlists brought across from another service, new themes. Installing and managing them is for administrators, from **Admin**, then **Plugins**. This needs the plugins permission, `server.plugins`, which administrators have.

<Callout title="How safe is a plugin?" tone="quiet">
  A plugin's code runs only on your server, in a sandbox of its own, and can reach only what you
  allow it. Nothing from a plugin ever runs in anyone's browser, phone or TV. See [how plugins are
  kept safe](/develop/plugins).
</Callout>

## The official plugins

| Plugin                       | What it does                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **AniList and MyAnimeList**  | Imports what each person has watched from AniList or MyAnimeList, marks those episodes watched in Valence, and keeps their lists up to date as they watch.                                     |
| **Trakt**                    | Imports each person's Trakt watch history, marks those films and episodes watched in Valence, and sends what they finish in Valence to Trakt.                                                  |
| **ListenBrainz and Last.fm** | Scrobbles what each person plays to ListenBrainz and Last.fm, and keeps their ListenBrainz Weekly Jams, Weekly Exploration and Daily Jams and their Last.fm loved tracks as Valence playlists. |
| **Playlist import**          | Brings Spotify and Apple Music playlists into Valence, and can request the albums of the songs your library doesn't have yet.                                                                  |

Their source is in [ValenceOSS/valence-plugins](https://github.com/ValenceOSS/valence-plugins). Each talks to its service through that service's own public API, and each needs an account or a key from it: the plugin's **Settings** say what to fill in and link to where to get it.

## From the catalogue

**Official plugins** lists the plugins in the catalogue. Each is signed by the Valence project, and your server checks the signature before it installs anything. Where the newest version of a plugin needs a newer Valence than yours, you're offered the newest version yours runs; where none does, it's marked **Needs a newer Valence**.

1. Choose a plugin, then **Install**.
2. Read what it asks to be allowed to do. Every permission is said in plain words, for example "Talks to api.spotify.com" or "Reads and updates what people have watched".
3. If you are happy, **Install**.

## From a file

**Install from a file** takes a `.vplugin` file, for a plugin someone gave you or one you wrote. Choose its `.sig` file with it if it came with one.

<Callout title="Unsigned plugins" tone="warning">
  If the file isn't signed by Valence or by a [key you trust](#trusting-other-signing-keys), Valence
  can't tell you who made it. It says so, and asks you to confirm you trust whoever gave it to you
  before installing. Only install an unsigned plugin from someone you would trust with what it asks
  for.
</Callout>

If the file is another version of a plugin you already have, Valence says which version it replaces, and points out anything it asks for that the installed one did not. If it's an older version, Valence says so, since what the newer one kept may not convert back.

Only a package Valence signed can replace a plugin from the official catalogue, so an unsigned file, or one signed by a key you trust, can't take over an official plugin, the data it kept or the accounts people connected to it. To replace one anyway, remove it first.

## Trusting other signing keys

A plugin's author can sign it with a key of their own. Trusting their key lets you install what it signs without the unsigned warning. It's still never called official: its badge says **Key you trust**, Valence hasn't checked it, and it can't replace an official plugin.

To trust a key, add it under **Trusted signing keys** on the Plugins page: its id, as the author named it, and its public key, the `.pub.pem` file the author shared or the line of text inside it. **Remove** beside a key stops trusting it, once you confirm. Plugins it signed stay installed as they are.

Keys can also be set with the `VALENCE_PLUGIN_TRUSTED_KEYS` [environment variable](/reference/environment-variables), as `id=key` pairs separated by commas. Those are listed as set by it and can't be removed from the page. A key can't take the id of one of Valence's own.

Pointing your server at a catalogue of your own with `VALENCE_PLUGIN_CATALOGUE_URL` works the same way: sign it with a key you trust, and its plugins are listed as **Plugins from a key you trust**.

Adding or removing a key is written to the audit log.

## Reading the permissions

| You will see                                          | It means                                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Talks to a list of addresses                          | It may make `https` requests to exactly those hosts, and nowhere else     |
| Reads your library                                    | It may search and read what is in your libraries, never change it         |
| Reads, or reads and updates, what people have watched | It may read, or also mark, watched progress for the people it acts for    |
| Asks for titles                                       | It may make requests, as the person using it and within their own rights  |
| Reads, or creates and fills, playlists                | It may read, or also make and add to, playlists of the people it acts for |
| Keeps its own data, up to a size                      | A private store for the plugin, removed with it                           |
| Connects to an account elsewhere                      | People may connect their account on that service to the plugin            |
| Sends notifications                                   | It may put notifications in people's bell                                 |
| Receives webhooks                                     | Outside services may send it messages at private addresses of its own     |
| Tells your webhooks things                            | It may send the events it lists to webhooks subscribed to plugin events   |

A plugin acts only for the people who open one of its pages or connect an account to it, and it hears about what they do, not anyone else. Seeing one of its panels on a title doesn't count. It never sees passwords, sessions or files, and it reads only the titles each of those people may see.

## Settings

Some plugins need details from you before they work, such as an OAuth client ID for a service people will connect to. Fill them in on the plugin's **Settings**. Secret settings are stored encrypted and never shown again once saved; type a new value to replace one.

A plugin that connects people's accounts on another service needs you to register an OAuth application with that service. Use this address as its redirect:

```
https://<your Valence address>/api/plugins/oauth/callback
```

The plugin's **Settings** shows this address under **Redirect URL**, ready to copy.

A plugin that receives webhooks lists one private address for each under **Settings** too. Give each to the service that sends it; anyone with the address can post to it, so treat it like a password. Reinstalling the plugin after removing it gives new addresses.

<Callout title="Addresses from outside" tone="warning">
  Both addresses are built from `BETTER_AUTH_URL`. For services outside your home to reach them, it
  must be the address Valence is reached at from the internet, not `localhost` or a local IP.
</Callout>

## Permissions a plugin adds

A plugin may add permissions of its own, such as "Sync lists", to decide who can use its pages and panels. They appear in the [role editor](/use/accounts-and-roles#the-permission-catalogue) in a group named after the plugin, and are granted like any other. The Administrator role holds them all. Removing the plugin takes its permissions out of every role, and installing it again does not bring those grants back.

## What people see

- **Pages** appear in each person's account area, or in the admin area for pages meant for administrators.
- **Panels** appear beside the things they belong to, such as a title or a playlist.
- **Themes** are offered with Valence's own on the theme choice, on the web, the phone and the TV.
- **Scheduled work** appears on the [jobs page](/use/jobs-and-logs), where you can run it now or see how it went. What a plugin logs appears on the logs page, tagged with its name.

## Updating

When the catalogue has a newer version your server runs, the plugin's row says so with **Update to** and the version. If the update asks for more than the installed version, you review the difference before it is installed.

Some updates convert what the plugin kept to a new shape as they install. While that runs, the plugin's row says **Updating**, people see that it is being updated where its pages and panels would be, and its scheduled work and events wait. Settings the new version still has keep their values; one it changed the kind of, say from words to a switch, starts empty.

### Rolling back

Valence keeps the version an update replaced, with its settings and what it had kept as they were at that moment. **Roll back to** and that version puts it back. Anything the plugin kept after the update is lost, and only one step back is kept: rolling back again, or updating again, starts afresh. A rollback is written to the audit log.

## Turning a plugin off, or removing it

- **The switch** on its row turns a plugin off at once. Its pages, panels and themes disappear and its scheduled work stops, but its settings, data and people's connected accounts are kept for when you turn it back on. Anybody on one of its pages, on the web, the phone or the TV, is taken back to where they came from and told why.
- **Remove** takes the plugin away with its settings, everything it kept, every connected account, its permissions in roles, its webhook addresses and the version kept for rolling back. The dialog first lists what this particular plugin would take with it, such as how much it kept and how many people connected an account to which service, and offers **Disable plugin** instead, which keeps all of that. Where a service supports it, Valence also asks that service to cancel the access people gave, so the plugin disappears from their account there too.

If a plugin fails, its row says **Plugin stopped working** and why. Valence waits before starting it again, a second at first and twice as long each time it fails again, up to five minutes, so a broken plugin can't keep the server busy. Turning it off and on again starts it afresh at once.
