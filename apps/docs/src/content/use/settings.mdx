The admin area is where you run the server. It lives at `/admin` and is split into groups of panels down the left: Overview at the top, then **Activity** (Sessions, Links, Statistics, Jobs & logs, Audit log), **Content** (Libraries, Media, Files, Encoding, Live TV), **People** (Accounts, Roles), **Requests** (Overview, Catalogue, Indexers, Quality profiles, Downloads) and **System** (Settings, Alerts, Email, Backups, Updates, Storage, Subtitles, Webhooks, Linked servers, Plugins, Import). What you see depends on your permissions, and the Requests group only appears where [requesting](/install/requesting) is set up. This page covers the Settings panel in detail, then the Overview and Encoding panels.

Every setting saves as you change it and answers with a small toast so you know it took. A name or a message you type saves a moment after you stop typing, and still saves if you leave the page first. Where a save fails the control goes back to its old value and the toast says what could not be saved. Changing settings needs the `server.settings` permission, which only the Administrator role has by default. See [Accounts and roles](/use/accounts-and-roles).

The Settings panel is four cards: **Server**, **Sign-in screen**, **Online services** and **Pre-roll**. Settings about converting and streaming video are on the **Encoding** panel, and the settings for the request pages are on the Requests group's panels. A row with an **About** button beside its name explains it at more length there.

## Announcements

The **Sessions** panel, under Activity, has a card that posts one message to everybody using the server, for planned downtime, a new library or anything else the household should know. Write up to 500 characters, choose how serious it is (**News**, **Warning** or **Urgent**, drawn in the quiet, warning and danger colours) and how long it stays up: until you take it down, an hour, a day or a week. **Post it** replaces whatever was up before.

It appears at once, over every page of the web and desktop apps and at the top of the phone app, for everybody already signed in, and for anybody who signs in while it is up. Each person can dismiss it on their own device; a new announcement shows again even to those who dismissed the last one. **Take it down** removes it for everybody. One that has run its time goes on its own, within a minute.

The announcement is also served at `GET /api/announcement`, and posted with `PUT /api/admin/announcement` or removed with `DELETE /api/admin/announcement` by an account that can change the server's settings.

## Server

The **Server** card says what the server is called and how it looks, for everybody, signed in or not.

- **Name** is what the browser tab, the bar's wordmark and the emails call the server. Emails are signed "Sent by" that name.
- **Description** is the line a search engine or a shared link shows.
- **Logo** stands in for Valence's mark in the bar and for the browser's icon. Upload a square PNG, JPEG, WebP or AVIF up to 2 MB; **Remove** puts Valence's back.
- **Accent colour** is what buttons and highlights are drawn in, and the colour of the button in emails. Words on it switch between dark and light to stay readable. **Reset** puts Valence's colour back. A theme somebody chooses from a plugin still has the last word on their own device.

An empty name or description goes back to Valence's. The toast reads "Brand saved." The brand is also served with the rest of the server's appearance at `GET /api/appearance`, set at `PUT /api/admin/brand`, and the logo is uploaded at `PUT /api/admin/brand/logo` and served at `/api/brand/logo`.

### Corners

Sets the corner radius of everything in Valence, for everybody who uses the server. All corners are drawn from one scaled value, so a single choice changes them together.

| Level   | Radius scale    |
| ------- | --------------- |
| Sharp   | 0, fully square |
| Soft    | 0.6             |
| Default | 1               |
| Round   | 1.6             |

Saving shows "Roundness saved."

### Age ratings

Different countries rate films differently. A 15 and an R are not the same thing, so Valence orders ratings within one country rather than pretending they map onto each other. This setting chooses whose ratings to read.

| Region                   | Certificates            |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------- |
| United Kingdom (default) | U, PG, 12, 15, 18       |
| United States            | G, PG, PG-13, R, NC-17  |
| Ireland                  | G, PG, 12A, 15A, 16, 18 |
| Australia                | G, PG, M, MA15+, R18+   |
| Germany                  | FSK 0, 6, 12, 16, 18    |
| France                   | 0, 12, 16, 18           |
| Netherlands              | 0, 6, 9, 12, 14, 16, 18 |
| Spain                    | 0, 7, 12, 16, 18        |

The same country's content advice is shown beside the certificate on a title's page and in the top corner for the first few seconds of playing it: **Contains** violence, strong language, sexual content, nudity, drug use, frightening scenes or discrimination, as the catalogue describes the title. Where that country says nothing, another country's advice is used, since what a film contains does not change at a border. Advice is read with the certificates when a title's details are fetched, so titles already in the library get it the next time their details are refreshed.

Every country's ratings are already stored, so changing country reads them again rather than rescanning your files. The toast reads "Certification region saved."

## Sign-in screen

### Show profiles

On by default. The way in draws the household's faces so somebody signs in by picking one. Turn it off and the way in asks for a username or an email address, and a password, instead, and nobody who has not signed in can read the names, pictures or identifiers of the people here. Consider turning it off on a server that is exposed to the internet. The toast reads "Sign-in screen saved."

### Background

An optional background drawn behind the faces on the sign in screen, with the colour of whoever is chosen washing over it. It only shows while **Show profiles** is on, because only somebody allowed to see the faces sees it. Accepted types are JPEG, PNG, WebP, AVIF and GIF, up to 16 MB. **Choose** one, **Replace** it, or **Remove** it. Toasts read "Splashscreen saved." and "Splashscreen removed.", and an upload that fails says why under the row.

### Message

A short message shown under **Who's watching?** on the sign in screen of the web app, the desktop app and the phone app, such as a welcome or the house rules. It can be up to 400 characters, and line breaks are kept. It saves a moment after you stop typing, with "Message saved."

**Message colour** draws it in one of the ready colours, or any other from the palette swatch, or in the theme's own text colour.

### Apps

Which of Valence's apps people can use with this server: **Desktop**, **Phone** and **TV**, each on by default. **Web** is always on, since it is how this page is reached. The toast reads "Saved which apps may connect."

### Sign-in isn't over HTTPS

This row only appears when sign-in cookies aren't marked secure, which means anyone on the network can read them. It lists the addresses allowed to sign in. Put Valence behind HTTPS to clear it; see [Configuration](/install/configuration) and [Reverse proxy](/install/reverse-proxy). If sign in fails from a particular address, check it is in that list.

## Online services

Everything Valence connects to outside this server, in two groups.

### API keys

Each key row shows whether a key is set. **Add key** opens a field for one; once set, entering a new one replaces it, and the stored key is never shown again.

- **The Movie Database** fetches titles, years, posters and descriptions for films and series. Without it, everything comes from filenames alone. The key can also be supplied at install time with the `CATALOGUE_API_KEY` environment variable (see [Configuration](/install/configuration)), and it is the default the setting starts from. The toast reads "Catalogue key saved."
- **OMDb** is optional. A free key adds each title's Rotten Tomatoes score as titles are scanned, so run a scan after saving. The toast reads "OMDb key saved."
- **fanart.tv** is optional. A personal key adds fanart.tv's posters, backgrounds and logos to a title's Artwork tab, beside TMDB's. The toast reads "fanart.tv key saved".
- **TheAudioDB** is optional too. Without a key, artists are looked up with the free public key, which finds only one music video for each artist. The toast reads "TheAudioDB key saved."

<Callout title="Keys are write only" tone="quiet">
  The Movie Database, OMDb, fanart.tv and TheAudioDB keys are stored on the server and only ever
  reported as set or not set. The panel cannot show them back to you, so keep your own copy.
</Callout>

### Features

- **Trailers.** Off by default. When on, Valence offers a trailer for titles that have none on disk, played in an embedded player from the video site the catalogue points at. This is the one thing Valence does that reaches outside your server for playback, which is why it is off until you say otherwise. A trailer already beside the file is always used instead. The toast reads "Trailer setting saved."
- **Music details.** Off by default. When on, Valence looks for what a music library's files left out: album covers on the Cover Art Archive, artists' photographs and music videos on TheAudioDB, and song words from LRCLIB. Each album, artist and song is asked about once, on the next scan, and nothing a file already carries is replaced. The toast reads "Music metadata setting saved."
- **Phone notifications.** Off by default. Phones only receive notifications through Apple's and Google's servers, so Valence's relay passes on a wake-up. It's told which phone to wake, never what the notification says; the phone reads that from this server.
- **Usage statistics.** Whether the server sends the Valence project one anonymous report a day: its release, the kind of machine it is on, and roughly how big its libraries are, with no names, titles, paths or addresses. It is off unless you turn it on, and the choice made in setup carries over. **See what's sent** opens the exact report as it would go next, and **Make a new install ID** inside it replaces the random ID the reports carry. Where `VALENCE_TELEMETRY=off` or `DO_NOT_TRACK=1` is set, the switch is off and can't be changed, and the row says it was turned off on this server by the environment. The toast reads "Usage statistics setting saved." Every field is listed on [Anonymous usage statistics](/use/telemetry).

## Pre-roll

**Play a clip first** plays a random clip from a folder before a title starts, such as your own intro or a cinema ident; viewers can skip it. **Plays before** chooses **Films**, **Episodes** or **Both**, and **Folder** is where the clips are: MP4, M4V or MOV files, sent as they are. The card says when Valence doesn't have permission to read the folder, or when there are no clips in it. The toast reads "Pre-roll settings saved".

## Encoding settings

The **Encoding** panel opens with two cards of settings, above pre-transcoding and re-encoding.

### Hardware acceleration

On the **Hardware** card. Valence normally picks whichever encoder the machine proves it can use. Choose one to insist on it, which also uses an encoder that failed that check, for the rare case where the check is wrong and the card plainly works.

| Choice              | Meant for                                   |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Automatic (default) | Use whichever the machine proves it can do. |
| VAAPI               | Intel and AMD on Linux.                     |
| QuickSync           | Intel.                                      |
| NVENC               | NVIDIA.                                     |
| AMF                 | AMD, needs the proprietary driver.          |
| VideoToolbox        | Apple.                                      |
| RKMPP               | Rockchip.                                   |
| Media Foundation    | Qualcomm on Windows on Arm.                 |
| Software only       | Never use the hardware.                     |

Saving shows "Hardware encoding saved." The Overview shows what was actually detected under Graphics.

**Test**, beside the choice, tries it on a short real transcode: five seconds of 1080p H.264, made smaller the way a phone's stream is, through the same pipeline a viewer's session uses. The result shows beneath it: whether the frames were decoded on the device, which encoder ran and how many times faster than real time it went, beside the same transcode in software. Where the hardware failed it says what ffmpeg reported, so a missing driver or an unshared device shows up here rather than in the logs. It tries what is chosen even before it is saved. The same test is `POST /api/admin/hardware/test`, with an optional `accel`. Getting a card through to the media service in the first place is covered in [Hardware transcoding](/install/hardware-transcoding).

**Use more than one graphics device**, below the test, spreads streams across every graphics device switched on in the list under it, filling the first before moving on to the next. Each device can be switched off, moved up or down, and given a **Most streams** limit. **When every device is full** says what a new stream does once each has as many as it may take: **Encode in software**, the default, or **Put it on the first device anyway**. With only one device found, it says so and there is nothing to choose. Saving shows "Graphics devices saved." See [Using more than one device](/install/hardware-transcoding#using-more-than-one-device).

### Streaming

The **Streaming** card holds four settings.

#### Short segments

On by default. Video is sent in pieces of about two seconds rather than four, so playback starts and seeks sooner over a slow connection or a tunnel, at the cost of more requests. The toast reads "Segment length saved."

#### Gapless episodes

On by default. In the last moments of an episode, Valence starts preparing the next one so it follows on without a wait.

#### Preview quality

Previews are the short clips that play when you hover a title. They are made once per file and kept, so this is a trade between disk and processor time and how good they look across a large hero image.

| Choice         | Clip width  | Notes                                                                       |
| -------------- | ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Low            | 854 pixels  | Smallest files, quickest to make. For a server short of space or processor. |
| Standard       | 1280 pixels | Sharp on a card, a little soft across a hero.                               |
| High (default) | 1920 pixels | Full width, at the best quality.                                            |

Changing it makes every preview again in the background, so expect a burst of work on a large library. Watch it in [Jobs and logs](/use/jobs-and-logs). A toast reads "Preview quality saved."

#### Fast thumbnails

Off by default, and shown only on a server with a GPU that isn't set to Software only. On such a server, seek-bar thumbnails are usually made from every frame of a file on the GPU, so each one shows the exact moment it is for. A file the GPU can't decode falls back to keyframes on the CPU. With this on, SDR files are made from keyframes only, on the CPU. That is several times faster and leaves the GPU alone, but keyframes can be ten seconds or more apart, so a thumbnail often shows a nearby shot rather than the moment you are pointing at. In our tests on 1080p episodes, about half to three quarters of thumbnails showed a different shot.

A server without a GPU already makes every thumbnail from keyframes on the CPU, which is why the switch isn't shown there. HDR files aren't affected by this setting: they are made as before. Turning it on or off doesn't redraw any thumbnails that already exist. To redraw a title's thumbnails, use the rebuild action in its menu. The toast reads "Fast thumbnails saved."

#### Keep prepared downloads

How long a file prepared for download is kept after it was last asked for, 14 days by default. Anyone who downloads the same title in that time gets the same file without waiting. The toast reads "Saved how long prepared downloads are kept."

## Request settings

Where [requesting](/install/requesting) is set up:

- **Background trailers**, on the **Request page** card of **Requests › Overview**, plays a muted trailer behind a title on its request page. It needs **Trailers** turned on in Settings › Online services.
- **Artist releases**, on the **Artist requests** card of **Requests › Quality profiles**, chooses which of an artist's records are fetched where whoever asked did not say. The default is albums, mixtapes and EPs, and it can be changed on any single request. See [Requests](/use/requests). The toast reads "Release types saved."

## Overview

The first panel of the admin area is a live view of the server.

![The admin Overview panel with the load chart, the Processor, Memory, Graphics and Storage strip and the Libraries, Watching now and Running now regions below.](/screenshots/admin-overview.png)

- **Concerns.** A banner at the top lists problems Valence has noticed and links to the panel that fixes each: the requests service or VPN being down, an indexer failing, the media service being unreachable, an FFmpeg that is too old, failed or stalled jobs, memory or the library disk nearly full, a processor or graphics encoder at full stretch, previews not being kept, libraries never scanned, no libraries, or no catalogue key. Each can be dismissed and stays dismissed until the concern changes. To stop one kind showing at all, turn it off under [Alerts](#alerts).
- **Setup guide.** On a fresh server the Overview offers a guide covering adding a library, setting a catalogue key and scanning. You can hide it. If there are no libraries you are taken to the Libraries panel once. See [First run](/use/first-run).
- **Stat strip.** Processor, memory, graphics and storage, each with how much is Valence's own. Hardware encoding, hardware chains and HDR conversion sit under Graphics.
- **Server load.** A chart of processor use over the last minute, with longer ranges available.
- **Watching now, Running now, Libraries and Recent jobs.** What is being streamed, which jobs are working, each library and its last scan, and how recent jobs went. See [Jobs and logs](/use/jobs-and-logs).

Reading the Overview needs `server.monitor`, held by Managers and Administrators.

### Alerts

**System → Alerts** has a switch for every kind of concern the Overview can show, grouped by what it is about: services, requests, playback, jobs, server resources and libraries. Turning one off hides it from the Overview for every administrator until it is turned on again; dismissing a concern only hides that one occurrence. Turning off a red alert, one about something broken, asks first. **Turn all on** puts every alert back. Each switch saves as it is flipped and shows "Alerts saved." Webhooks are not affected: they keep sending what they are set up to send.

### Storage Valence is using

This region breaks down what Valence keeps on disk apart from your media: preview clips, scrub thumbnails, transcode sessions, artwork and book pages, plus the size of the media library itself. Counting these is slow, so the figures come from the last count. Press **Refresh** to count again. A toast reads "Counted the storage again." If the count fails it says the storage could not be counted. Refreshing needs `server.monitor`.

## Encoding and re-encoding

Re-encoding converts a media file into a smaller or more compatible one. It is the one thing in Valence that can destroy your own media, so it has its own permission, `media.reencode`, which no default role except Administrator holds, and its own safeguards.

### Starting one

1. Open the **Media** panel, open the row menu on a title and choose **Re-encode**. Or open **Encoding** and press **Re-encode a title**.
2. Filter by library, search by title or set a minimum size in GB, then tick the files.
3. Choose what to do.
4. Read the estimate. Nothing starts until you press the button at the bottom, and a final confirmation repeats what will happen.

| Mode           | What happens                                                                         |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Replace        | Frees disk. The original is kept until you have watched the result and confirmed it. |
| Keep alongside | Costs disk, and means the server never has to convert that title on the fly.         |
| Audio only     | Copies the picture untouched and only compresses lossless audio. The safest option.  |

For Replace and Keep alongside you also choose a quality step (from 144p up to 4K, with a maximum bitrate for each) and a codec (H.264, HEVC or AV1). The **Compress the lossless audio too** option shrinks a TrueHD or DTS-HD track, which is often a large share of a remux, and narrows 7.1 to 5.1.

Keep alongside also asks three more things. **Container format** keeps the original's container, or makes the copy an MP4 or an MKV; an MP4 copy leaves out subtitles, chapters and any audio an MP4 cannot hold is compressed, while the original keeps them all. **Save copy to** is Valence's own `.valence` folder at the top of the library, or beside the original under a readable name such as `Film (2016) - 1080p H264.valence.mp4`. **Bitrate ceiling** holds the copy to fewer kilobits a second than the quality step allows. To make copies of a whole library automatically, overnight, use [Pre-transcoding](/use/pre-transcoding).

Copies kept alongside are listed, and can be removed, from **Saved copies** in a title's menu in **Media**. Removing one deletes its file.

### What Valence refuses

Before anything starts Valence checks each file and tells you why it will not proceed, so a Friday batch does not fail overnight.

| Refusal                     | Reason                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Already queued              | It is already queued or being worked on.                                                                                       |
| Being watched               | Replacing a file under a live stream would end it.                                                                             |
| Folder is read only         | Valence cannot write to the folder. Media is often mounted read only, which is sensible; mount it read and write to re-encode. |
| Subtitles would not survive | An AVI or M2TS file carries subtitles its container cannot hold, and the container is never changed.                           |
| Already kept                | A copy just like this one is already kept beside the film.                                                                     |
| Name is taken               | A file Valence did not make already has the name a copy kept beside the film would take. It is never written over.             |
| Already as small            | The result would match or exceed what the file is already, so it would waste a generation of quality.                          |
| Not enough room             | The estimate does not fit in free space.                                                                                       |
| Too many awaiting review    | The review queue is full.                                                                                                      |

### Review, then decide

When an encode finishes it does not replace anything yet. It appears under **Waiting for your review** in the Encoding panel, with its size before and after. Press **Review** to compare frames from the original and the encode, then either confirm or reject it.

- Confirming keeps the new encode and removes the original: "Kept the new encode and removed the original."
- Rejecting puts the original back in one action: "Put the original back."
- Stopping a running encode says "Stopped the re-encode."

Because every waiting encode holds a film and its replacement on disk, the queue pauses when too many encodes are waiting for you (five by default). Review some to let it carry on. Starting shows "Started re-encoding."

![The Encoding panel showing Waiting for you with a Review button, an Under way list with progress bars and the Already done table.](/screenshots/admin-encoding.png)

An encode moves through Waiting its turn, Encoding, Checking what came out, Waiting for you and then Done. Rejected, Failed and Stopped are the other outcomes. The **Completed** table keeps the history.
