The phone app is Valence for iPhone and Android. It is the same library, the same household and the same progress as the web app, laid out for a hand rather than a pointer and drawn with the phone's own controls. Like the [desktop app](/install/desktop-app), it is a window onto your server: it keeps no library of its own beyond what you download to it.

## Finding your server

The first time it opens, the app asks where your Valence is. On the same network as the server, it lists the servers it hears announcing themselves, so you can pick yours rather than type it. Otherwise type the address you use in a browser, with or without `https://`; the app tries it before keeping it and says so if nothing answers. You can point the phone at another server later from the account page.

## Signing in

The app then shows the wall of faces from your server. Choose yours and sign in with your password, followed by your authenticator's code if the account has two-step sign in, or with a passkey, which the app offers beside the faces. See [Accounts and roles](/use/accounts-and-roles#your-own-account).

A phone can also sign a television in: press the code button in the corner of the home page, scan the code the television shows, and approve it in the app.

## The tabs

| Tab      | What it holds                                                                                                                       |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Home     | The featured titles and the rows of the web's home page, with films, programmes, music and books a swipe away along the top.        |
| Search   | Every library at once, and for somebody who may ask for things, the catalogue: what is trending and coming, and what you asked for. |
| Calendar | What comes out over the coming weeks: new episodes of programmes in your libraries, and the films and programmes you asked for.     |
| Account  | Your profile, your downloads, requests and inbox, how you sign in, what you have watched and hidden, and the links you have shared. |

A title's page carries what the web's does: its facts, certificate and quality badges, the synopsis, cast and trailer, and the buttons to play, keep, download, share and hide. A programme's page is read season by season.

## Downloads and watching offline

Download a film or an episode from its page, choosing a quality against its size first. The server prepares the file and the phone fetches it; a fetch can be paused and picked up again, and one that drops carries on where it stopped. Downloads, under Account, shows how far each has got and how fast.

Albums, playlists, your liked songs and audiobooks download from their pages too. For music you choose a quality first, from lossless down to data saver, each with roughly how much room it takes, and an iPhone is sent a copy it can play where the file itself is one it cannot. Nothing has to be prepared, so the songs start arriving at once, one after another, and the button on the page says how many are here. Press it again to pause, carry on, download it again to pick up songs added to a playlist since, or take it off the phone. A song in both an album and a playlist you downloaded is kept once.

What the phone keeps plays with no server at all, in the app, on the lock screen and in CarPlay, where a Downloaded tab lists the music on the phone. Where you got to in an audiobook while offline is sent to the server once it can be reached again. When the server cannot be reached the app says so, keeps trying on its own, and still lists and plays everything downloaded. How long the server keeps a prepared film is an administrator's setting in the admin area.

## Watching and listening

The player has its own controls for a phone: quality, audio, subtitles and their timing, and speed, fading away while you watch. Music plays in a bar above the tabs that opens into a full player with the queue and lyrics, and audiobooks have a player of their own with the same chapters, speeds and sleep timer as the web's. See [Audiobooks](/use/audiobooks).

## Playing on another screen

From the music player, choose where the music plays: this phone, or any other Valence your profile has open, such as a browser, the desktop app, a television or another phone. It carries on from the same moment, with the rest of the queue, and the phone becomes its remote. On an iPhone the system's AirPlay button sends the sound to speakers and televisions as well.

To watch a film or a programme on a television, press the TV button in the corner of its page and choose the television. It starts there from where you got to, the television says for a moment which phone started it, and the phone opens as its remote.

The phone becomes the remote by itself, too, whenever a television on your profile starts playing, with a bar above the tabs that opens into the full remote. It has the line through the film with how far in it is, ten seconds back and on, the previous and next episode on a programme or the previous and next chapter on a film, and the offer to skip the opening or the credits while the television makes it. Beneath are the television's own speed, sound, subtitles and quality. **Play here** brings the film back to the phone from the same moment, and the stop button in the corner ends it on the television. Swipe the bar down to put the remote away until the television starts something new.

## Getting the app

On an iPhone or iPad, get [Valence from the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6815748526). The same download is the app for Apple TV, and Safari on an iPhone offers it above the web app, opening it straight to your server once it is installed.

The Android app is not on Google Play yet. Until it is, it is built from the `apps/mobile` folder of the [repository](https://github.com/ValenceOSS/Valence) with Expo and installed from there.
